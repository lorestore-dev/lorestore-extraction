## Needs a human
- Oliver's likeness to the portrait is stated by the narrator ("its living copy"), so
  it's recorded as a fact of his appearance. Who the lady is isn't said; it's left
  unrecorded, and no secret is proposed for it yet.
- Scenes 3 and 4 go back in time: the boys' flight happens at OT:10:3, days before
  scene 1. Their rows are dated at OT:12:3 and OT:12:4, where the unit tells them, not
  where they happen. Should a flashback within a unit be dated at the earlier point
  (an order inversion, §6.8)?
- The portrait is an object with no place to go except a place's description.
- The narrator again calls Fagin "the Jew", and "the merry old gentleman". See ch08.
